Liberalising Kenya’s communications sector more than two decades ago boosted the roll-out of communications infrastructure and services, but licensed commercial operators have tended to concentrate where a return on investment is guaranteed.

Areas seen as commercially unviable have remained unserved or underserved. The Kenya Information and Communications (Amendment) Act, 2009, provides for a Universal Service Fund (USF), administered and managed by the Authority, to support widespread access to ICT services and promote capacity building and innovation. The Fund is financed by levies on licensees, government appropriations, and grants and donations, and finances national projects with significant impact on the availability and accessibility of ICTs in rural, remote and poor urban areas.

The Authority carried out ICT Access Gap studies in 2016 and 2021 to establish how public institutions and enterprises in Kenya access and use ICTs, and has since carried out several projects to close the gaps.

Projects closing the gaps

  1. Voice infrastructure projects
    Identifying unserved and underserved sub-locations for mobile network coverage. The first phase connected 78 sub-locations, the second phase targets 101, and a third phase is planned for a further 67.

  2. Education Broadband Connectivity Project
    Provided 5 Mbps internet services to 884 public secondary schools across all 47 counties.

  3. E-resource centres
    With the Kenya National Library Service (KNLS), supporting 56 public libraries with computers, internet connectivity and accessibility software, turning them into e-resource centres.

  4. Open education resources
    Working with the Kenya Institute of Curriculum Development (KICD) to digitise education content and narrow the education divide through an online portal.
