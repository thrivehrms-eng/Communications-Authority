To make timely, informed interventions towards universal access, the Authority carries out studies and research on a continuous basis.

Internet Market Study

The Internet Market Analysis study recognised the growing importance of the internet to socio-economic development. Focusing on the supply side, it created baseline information on the internet market in Kenya, identified internet penetration and the factors behind it, and made policy and regulatory recommendations to grow the sub-sector.

National ICT Survey

With the Kenya National Bureau of Statistics (KNBS), the Authority carried out a comprehensive national survey of ICT access and usage. Its findings inform progressive policy guidelines, access programmes, projects and strategies.

The main objective was to collect, collate and analyse data on ICT access and usage by different groups in Kenya, from primary and secondary sources. The survey captured data on key internationally recognised ICT indicators for households, individuals, institutions and businesses, broken down by age, gender, youth, administrative region, and rural and urban location. It is the basis of a detailed ICT access and usage database, to be updated every year.

Access Gaps Study

To address low penetration of ICT services, the Government created the Universal Service Fund (USF) through the Kenya Information and Communications (Amendment) Act, 2013. The Fund is expected to leverage operations in high-cost areas, support innovation in the sector and increase ICT capacity in the country.

To give the Fund a strong, empirical foundation, the Authority commissioned an Access Gaps Study. Its main objective was to develop the most appropriate framework, based on international best practice, for providing affordable ICT services (telephony, internet, postal services and broadband) to all parts of the country, including rural and high-cost areas. The framework complements, and does not replace, the effective operation of market forces. Its specific objectives included:

  • Reviewing current and planned universal access programmes by the Government, operators and other stakeholders in the ICT sector, for their impact, how well they meet demand, and the cost and sustainability of the investment.
  • Analysing demand and supply for ICT services, including telephony, internet, postal and courier services and broadband, in unserved areas, with emphasis on the services in greatest demand.
  • Evaluating international best practice in universal access programmes, focusing on what applies best in Kenya.
  • Developing a financial model to estimate subsidies for identified universal access areas and programmes, to achieve the greatest impact at the best cost-benefit ratio.
