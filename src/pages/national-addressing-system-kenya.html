The National Addressing System of Kenya (NASK) is the latest system the Authority is developing under its numbering and addressing mandate.

It is a framework for naming and numbering streets, and numbering properties such as buildings and parcels of land, so that a parcel or dwelling can be identified and located on the ground.

Legal background

The Kenya Information and Communications (Numbering) Regulations, 2010, require the Authority, among other things, to:

  1. Establish a National Communications and Addressing Plan (NCAP) for electronic communication numbers and addresses, and postal codes, in liaison with international organisations dealing with numbering and addressing.
  2. Make sure the NCAP includes, among other details, geographical postal points of delivery.
  3. Appoint, if necessary, a person or organisation to manage or maintain an integrated number or address database.
  4. Assign addresses and communication numbers based on the NCAP and, if satisfied, issue a certificate on application.

Implementation

To establish a NASK for the country, the Authority hosted two stakeholder buy-in workshops in 2016. The first, on 18 March 2016, gathered comments and suggestions from stakeholders, particularly counties, on the proposed framework, and a NASK Committee was constituted from the stakeholders identified.

The workshop was attended by 100 participants from the Authority, county governments, the Kenya Revenue Authority, the Kenya Bureau of Standards, the Ministry of Information, Communication and Technology, the Courier Industry Association of Kenya (CIAK), the Higher Education Loans Board (HELB), Co-op Bank, Standard Chartered Bank, the Council of Governors (CoG), the Postal Corporation of Kenya, the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC), the Kenya Urban Roads Board, the Ministry of Transport and Infrastructure, Survey of Kenya and the National Land Commission, among others.

The workshop recommended a multi-stakeholder Steering Committee, led by the Authority, to drive the process. The Steering Committee held five meetings and hosted a second buy-in workshop on 24 October 2016, attended by senior county officials, the ICT Cabinet and Principal Secretaries, the Director General of the Universal Postal Union (UPU) and officials from other ministries. There, stakeholders including county officials were introduced to the NASK concept, its legal provisions, its importance and the roles of the various players.

Milestones

  1. March 2016

    Inaugural NASK stakeholder workshop recommends an inter-ministerial NASK Steering Committee.

  2. June 2016

    NASK Steering Committee constituted.

  3. July 2016

    Universal Postal Union technical assistance (consultancy mission) on a recommended NASK roadmap for Kenya.

  4. September 2016

    Draft NASK roadmap prepared, and procurement started for consultancy in four areas: policy, financial model, standards, and database development and management.

  5. October 2016

    Second buy-in workshop and inauguration of the national NASK Steering Committee.

  6. April 2017

    UPU certification of Kenya on the S42 addressing standards.

  7. September 2017

    Draft NASK standards and other guiding frameworks developed: property and street naming and numbering standards, the NAS policy, a county addressing law, a database management framework and financial modelling.

Next steps

  1. County engagement and sensitisation on the NAS framework.
  2. Validation of the NAS framework.
  3. Consideration and adoption of the NAS standards, and their incorporation in the Authority’s National Communications Numbering and Addressing Plan (NCNAP).
  4. Awareness among legislators, and adoption of NAS legislation.
  5. A communications strategy for implementing the project.
  6. Implementation in the counties.
  7. Development of a national database.
  8. Monitoring and evaluation by the NAS Secretariat and County Addressing Units (CAUs), reporting to the National Assembly.
  9. Policy review every three to five years, to keep pace with global and national trends.

The project will support the growth of e-commerce by improving the traceability of ICT transactions and confidence in using ICT services, and will enhance security and identity in the country.
