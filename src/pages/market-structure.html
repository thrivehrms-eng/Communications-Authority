The Authority regulates seven market segments, each with its own licence categories, fee schedules and licence terms and conditions.

Broadcasting

The Communications Authority of Kenya is mandated to license all broadcasting service providers under the Kenya Information and Communications (Amendment) Act, 2013. Under section 46C of the Kenya Information and Communications Act, 1998, it is illegal to provide any form of broadcasting service in Kenya without a licence; contravention attracts a fine of up to KSh 1,000,000 or imprisonment for up to three years, or both.

The Act envisages a broadcasting market structure of the following licence categories:

  • Public Broadcasting Services.
  • Community Broadcasting Services.
  • Private or Commercial Broadcasting Service.
  • Subscription Broadcasting Services.
  • Signal Distribution Services.
  • Any other class of licence as may be determined in accordance with the regulations.

Licence terms and conditions

The Authority has revised the licence terms and conditions of the following broadcasting licences in line with the current market structure, and has engaged stakeholders on a complaints handling procedure for broadcast content under its consumer protection mandate.

Telecommunication

Development of large-scale telecommunications infrastructure in Kenya, capable of delivering efficient and affordable info-communications services, is a critical pre-requisite for the country’s economic growth. The Authority is responsible for the development and implementation of policies and strategies for telecommunications services, licensing operators and service providers, and monitoring their performance to ensure compliance with the Kenya Information and Communications Act, 1998, and its regulations.

Application forms and fees are on the Telecommunications Services page.

The current telecommunications market structure took effect in April 2026, based on a Unified Licensing Framework (ULF) operating on technology and service-neutrality principles, with five broad categories and 19 distinct licence categories:

  • National Infrastructure Licences – Network Facilities Provider Tier 1, Tier 2 and Tier 3.
  • National Infrastructure & Service Licences – Micro Network and Service Provider, Community Network and Services Provider, Private VSAT (operated through foreign hub operators).
  • International Infrastructure & Service Licences – Landing Rights Authorization, International Gateway Systems and Services.
  • Service Licences – Applications Service Provider, Content Service Provider, Electronic Certification Service Provider, Dot KE Sub-domain Name Registrar Service Provider, Communications Equipment Distributor/Contractor/Vendor, Public Communications Access Centre, Business Process Outsourcing.
  • Technical Personnel Licences – Telecommunications Engineering Personnel, Telecommunications Systems Professionals.

Postal and courier

Under the Kenya Information and Communications Act, 1998, the Authority is responsible for postal services in the following ways:

  • Ensuring that postal and other related services are provided throughout Kenya on such terms as the Authority deems expedient.
  • Ensuring the public post licensee can provide postal services at rates consistent with efficient, continuous service and financial viability.
  • Promoting development of postal systems and services in line with recognised international standards, practices and public demand.
  • Exercising licensing and regulatory functions over postal systems and services in Kenya.
  • Regulating the fixing of postage rates and other fees charged for postal services.
  • Promoting and developing standards in postal systems and services.

Frequency spectrum

Managing the country’s radio frequency spectrum and numbering resources is one of the Authority’s primary responsibilities, entailing planning and assignment of spectrum to radio communications services and users, national co-ordination for harmonious frequency sharing, and international/regional co-ordination to avoid harmful interference. Services requiring frequency licences include TV and FM radio broadcasting, cellular mobile telecommunications, satellite communications, aeronautical and maritime radio services, and emergency/disaster communications. The Authority inspects and monitors assigned spectrum to ensure it is used in line with licence terms.

Under section 36 of the Kenya Information and Communications Act, 1998, all radio communication equipment in Kenya must be owned and/or operated under a licence issued by the Authority, kept in force by regular payment of the prescribed fee. Licence types include:

  • Private Radio Networks (HF/VHF, Private Paging).
  • Fixed, Cellular Mobile and Public Paging Services.
  • Amateur Radio Licence.
  • Citizen Band Radio Licence.
  • Aircraft Station Licence.
  • Maritime (Ship) Station Licence.

The Authority is implementing spectrum sharing as a key regulatory intervention to accommodate rising spectrum demand, including authorising the use of TV white spaces (TVWS) in the 470–694 MHz UHF band where specific channels are unused for Digital Terrestrial Television broadcasting, controlled by geolocation databases. The Qualified Geolocation Database listing is maintained separately. Frequencies are planned every three to four years following the ITU World Radiocommunication Conference, which revises the Radio Regulations on an agenda set by the ITU Council.

Type approval

The Authority is responsible for type-approving and accepting all telecommunications and radio communication equipment intended for use in Kenya’s public telecommunications networks. Type approval is done once for each equipment model, and covers items such as mobile cellular devices (2G–5G and future generations), PSTN equipment (PABX, fax, telephone handsets), radio communication equipment, data equipment (routers, modems, switches) and digital set-top boxes.

Any entity wishing to market, sell or use communication equipment in Kenya must seek type approval or acceptance. Only duly licensed vendors and contractors may market or sell such equipment, and must obtain type approval for each model before doing so. Applicants should check the List of Type Approved Equipment (and rejected equipment) before applying, and submit the Application Form for Type Approval/Acceptance together with a letter of agency from the manufacturer or principal distributor, a sample of the equipment, technical and user manuals in English, conformance certificates and test reports from an accredited laboratory, a valid copy of the Authority’s licence/compliance evidence, and proof of payment of the non-refundable fee.

The Authority uses the laboratory and manufacturer’s test reports and technical documentation to evaluate submitted equipment; it is under no obligation to return this documentation. If the equipment complies with the mandatory standards, a Provisional Type Approval or Authority to Use is granted; if the application is rejected, the applicant is informed.

Numbering

In 2002 the Authority unveiled a telecommunications numbering and addressing scheme to ensure stability and a steady supply of numbers for end users, necessitated by rapid growth following market liberalisation. Efficient management and administration of numbering resources remains central to this mandate.

Eligibility

  • All licensed operators that require numbers for their operations are eligible for assignment from the national numbering resource.
  • Organisations intending to become licensed, where the licence has been agreed in principle, may seek reservations for planning purposes for up to three months.
  • End users and service providers requiring numbers seek secondary assignment from eligible holders, on a transparent, non-discriminatory and equitable basis.
  • All licensed mobile operators may administer Short Numbers with leading digits 2–8 for their own customers’ text messaging, subject to audit.
  • Short codes beginning with 1 and 9 are reserved for customer services and emergency services and are assigned only directly by the Authority.

The National Numbering Plan, prepared and periodically updated under the Kenya Information and Communications Act, 1998, sets out the principles, rules and guidelines for managing numbering resources for all telecom services in Kenya, remaining consistent with ITU recommendations and the principles of transparency and equity. As numbers are a limited national resource, they must be used efficiently and managed to meet current and future demand.

Electronic certification providers

An Electronic Certification Service Provider (E-CSP) is an entity licensed to issue, verify, store, renew, suspend and revoke digital certificates to subscribers securely. An E-CSP may begin issuing digital certificates only once accredited by the Authority, achieved by passing an initial regulatory compliance audit within one year of the grant of an E-CSP licence. Consumers should seek digital certificate services only from accredited E-CSPs.
