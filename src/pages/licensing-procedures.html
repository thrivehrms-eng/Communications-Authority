The Authority has procedures for each category of licence. Prospective licensees should follow the procedure for the licence they are applying for.

Postal, courier and telecommunications

The licensing process starts with a duly completed application form, completed in English and addressed to the Authority. Application forms are on the Telecommunications Services page; each form sets out the minimum requirements for an application to be accepted.

Once a duly completed application is accepted:

  1. Pay the application fee

    The prescribed application fee is non-refundable.

  2. Committee review

    The Communication Licensing Committee (CLC) considers the application.

  3. Public notice

    If the CLC endorses the application, it is published in the Kenya Gazette for at least 30 days, during which interested parties may make representations.

  4. Board decision

    When the Gazette notice period ends, the application goes to the Board. If it is approved, the applicant is advised of the licence offer conditions, including the licence fees payable.

  5. Licence issued

    The licence is issued once the conditions in the offer letter are met.

Broadcasting

Licensing of broadcasting services under the regulatory framework works in two ways:

  1. First come, first served applies to all broadcasting service licences that do not need a frequency to be assigned (such as cable TV, subscription management and landing rights).
  2. For broadcasting services that need frequency spectrum, the Authority first identifies the available frequencies and then advertises them publicly, in whole or in batches, for interested parties to apply. Applications that do not respond to such an advert or notice are not considered.

For frequency-based broadcasting services, the Authority publishes the list of available frequencies or channels in at least one print medium in Kenya and on its website. That publication is the point at which interested applicants submit applications for specific licences, within the time given.

Frequency spectrum

Under section 36 of the Kenya Information and Communications Act, 1998, all radio communication equipment in Kenya must be owned or operated under a licence issued by the Authority, kept in force at all times by regular payment of the prescribed licence fee.

Authorisation and licence administration covers:

  • Private radio networks (HF/VHF, private paging)
  • Fixed, cellular mobile and public paging services
  • Amateur radio licences
  • Citizen band radio licences
  • Aircraft station licences
  • Maritime (ship) station licences
  • Use of wireless access systems (WAS) on a shared, non-protected basis in the 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz frequency bands

Numbering resources

Who is eligible

  • All licensed operators that need numbers for their operations are eligible for assignment of numbers from the national numbering resource.
  • Organisations intending to become licensed, whose licence has been agreed in principle and who need numbers for planning, may reserve numbering resources for up to three months.
  • End users and service providers that need numbers seek secondary assignment from those eligible, on transparent, non-discriminatory and equitable terms.
  • All licensed mobile operators may administer short numbers with leading digits 2 to 8 for their own customers licensed to provide text messaging services to mobile users. These short numbers are subject to audit.
  • Short codes beginning with 1 and 9 are reserved for customer services and emergency services, and are assigned only by the Authority.

Conditions of assignment

  • Assigned numbers must be put into service within six months of assignment. Numbers not activated in that time, without the Authority’s approval, are withdrawn and made available to other users, and the assignee loses the right to use them.
  • Applicants for additional numbers must account for the numbers already assigned in that category and show evidence of 80% utilisation.
  • Numbers and addresses assigned by the Authority cannot be transferred without its permission.
  • The Authority may audit the use of numbering resources to make sure they are used for the intended purpose and in line with the prescribed procedures.
  • All assignments, reservations, reclamations and penalties are subject to the Kenya Communications (Amendment) Act, 2009, the regulations in force, licence conditions, the prescribed dialling formats and relevant International Telecommunication Union (ITU) standards and guidelines.
  • Any violation may result in the assignment being cancelled.

Forms for numbering resources are on the Telecommunications Services page.
