Overview

Protecting you as a consumer of ICT services.

The Communications Authority of Kenya (CA) is charged with protecting and safeguarding the interests of consumers in the provision of ICT services and equipment, in line with the Constitution of Kenya, 2010 (sections 46 and 47).

Consumer complaints

How to make a consumer complaint.

Consumers have the right to complain about their inability to access and use communication services, and the Authority is empowered to handle complaints against licensed communication service providers.

  1. Complain to your provider first

    You must first report the complaint to your service provider.

  2. Your provider responds

    A licensee must advise you at the time of your complaint of the expected action and timing for investigation and resolution, and inform you of the outcome and any decision made.

  3. Escalate within the provider

    If you are not satisfied with that decision, the licensee must offer an escalation process within its own organisation.

  4. Refer it to the Authority

    If it is still unresolved, report it to the Authority in writing within six months of the incident. The Authority will acknowledge it within three days of receipt and respond substantively within ten days.

Complaints about public service

Public complaints and misconduct.

What counts as a public complaint

The Commission on Administrative Justice (CAJ) defines a public complaint as an expression of dissatisfaction about an action, lack of action, or the standard of a service provided by a public institution or a person acting on its behalf, including corruption, lack of service, unethical conduct, maladministration, unfair decisions, misuse of office, delay, incompetence or discourtesy.

If a matter of this kind involves the Authority itself and cannot be resolved directly, it can ultimately be taken to the CAJ (the Ombudsman).

Report corruption or misconduct

To report suspected corruption or misconduct involving the Authority, its staff, or a licensed operator, use the confidential reporting form. Reports are anonymous by default.

The Authority’s Consumer Protection and Advocacy (CPA) Department can also be reached by email at complaints@ca.go.ke, or by phone on extensions 420, 426, 427 and 428.

Questions

Frequently asked questions.

What is a consumer complaint?

An expression of dissatisfaction by a user of ICT services regarding a service or product offered by a licensed ICT service provider.

Do I need to complain to my service provider first?

Yes. Try to resolve the issue with your provider before escalating it to the Authority.

When do I escalate to the Authority?

If the problem remains unresolved, or the provider is unresponsive or unsatisfactory, you may escalate the complaint.

What issues can I raise?

Poor network coverage, service interruption, billing or tariff disputes, delayed or failed service, unfair contract terms, fraudulent calls or SMS, unsolicited messages, frequency interference, defective equipment, misleading advertising, cybercrime, and postal/courier or broadcasting service issues, among others.

What should I include?

Your name and contact details, a description of the issue, relevant dates and times, any reference numbers, and copies of supporting documents.

What happens after I submit a complaint?

You receive an acknowledgment and a reference number, the complaint is investigated, and you are contacted with the outcome.

How long does resolution take?

Typically 1–30 days depending on complexity; you should be kept informed of progress throughout.

Is there a cost?

No. Filing a complaint is free of charge, and reports of corruption or misconduct may be made anonymously.

What if I am not satisfied with the outcome?

You may request a review by the Authority, or seek redress from the Commission on Administrative Justice, the Communications and Multimedia Appeals Tribunal, or a court of law, unless the matter is already before a court.
