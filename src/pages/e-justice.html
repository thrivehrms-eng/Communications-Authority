The Authority supports the digitalisation of the justice sector through the Universal Service Fund (USF).

Under the Judiciary Automation Project, the Authority and the Judiciary deployed Local Area Networks (LANs) in 42 court stations across 28 counties, at a cost of KES 250 million. The project aims to facilitate court automation, improve access to justice and make court processes more transparent, efficient and effective, particularly for underserved and unserved communities.

A local area network links the computers and devices within a court station, so data can be shared and communication can take place securely and instantly. The project is aligned with the Judiciary’s Social Transformation through Access to Justice (STAJ) blueprint and with the Government’s Digital Transformation Agenda.

What the project enables

  • Electronic case management, including e-filing and case tracking
  • Virtual court sessions through secure video links, including bail applications, plea-taking and full trials
  • Faster sharing of information within court stations
  • Less need for court users to travel long distances to attend court in person, saving time and costs

Launch

The project was launched on 13 June 2025 at the Msambweni Law Courts in Kwale County. The launch was presided over by Hon. Lady Justice Martha Koome, Chief Justice and President of the Supreme Court of Kenya, alongside Hon. William Kabogo, Cabinet Secretary for Information, Communications and the Digital Economy.

The courts are in Kirinyaga, Meru, Laikipia, Isiolo, Wajir, Garissa, Turkana, Bungoma, Nandi, Vihiga, Kakamega, Siaya, Kilifi, Mombasa, Kwale, Taita-Taveta, Makueni, Machakos, Marsabit, Baringo, Kitui, Embu, Tharaka-Nithi, Narok, Kisii, Migori, Bomet and Nyamira counties.

Capacity building

In March and April 2025, the Authority provided capacity building to more than 300 participants at the Magistrates and Kadhis Colloquium organised by the Kenya Judiciary Academy under the theme “Digital Transformation, Technology and the Law – Tech Justice”. The sessions covered emerging issues and technologies such as artificial intelligence, blockchain applications, cybersecurity, digital evidence and data protection.
